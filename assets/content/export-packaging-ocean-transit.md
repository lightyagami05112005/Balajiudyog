# Packing hardware and glass for the 35-day ocean haul

Damage doesn't happen on the showroom shelf. It happens in the 35 days between the Mundra warehouse and
an African distributor's floor — in the humidity of the hold, the drop at a transhipment port, the
forklift at the destination CFS. Packaging engineered for that journey is the difference between a 0.4%
damage rate and the industry's ~2%. Here is the protocol we run, and why each stage exists.

## The four-stage protocol

**Stage 1 — Inner protection (per piece).**
Individual wrapping sized to the product's fragility: tissue for finished brass, foam or air-cell for
glass and chandeliers. **Anti-rust treatment** on steel hardware; **anti-tarnish/lacquer** on brass.
**Humidity sachets** on hygroscopic items. This stage fights the two silent enemies of an ocean
container: moisture and abrasion.

**Stage 2 — Inner cartons.**
5-ply corrugated inner cartons with internal dividers, sized for **one-person handling** at an African
distributor's warehouse (heavy master cartons get damaged because they get dropped). Cartons are
weight-balanced and labelled to the packing list.

**Stage 3 — Outer cartons / crates.**
7-ply outer cartons for general hardware; **ISPM-15 fumigated wooden crates** for fragile and heavy
items (chandeliers, large brass, glass panels). Steel-strapped and corner-protected. ISPM-15 compliance
matters — non-compliant wood packaging is rejected at African ports.

**Stage 4 — Container loading.**
**Block-and-brace** loading so cargo can't shift in a rolling hold. Desiccants placed per CBM to control
condensation ("container rain"). Heavy at the bottom, fragile braced and isolated. A **container photo
manifest** is taken at door-close and sent to the buyer on dispatch day — both a trust document and a
claims baseline.

## Why each detail earns its place

- **Anti-tarnish/lacquer + desiccant:** humidity in a steel box swings hard between day and night;
  unprotected brass arrives dull and steel arrives spotted. This is the most common avoidable claim.
- **One-person inner cartons:** the damage you don't see at origin happens at destination handling. Size
  for the receiving warehouse, not the shipping dock.
- **ISPM-15 crates:** protects fragile cargo *and* clears phytosanitary rules — two problems, one
  solution.
- **Block-and-brace + desiccant:** stops shift damage and condensation, the two transit-specific risks a
  showroom-grade pack ignores.
- **Photo manifest:** resolves disputes fast and signals a serious operation.

## What an importer should ask for

1. The **packing specification** for fragile lines (crate vs carton, ply, dividers).
2. **Anti-rust / anti-tarnish** treatment confirmed on metals.
3. **Desiccant per CBM** and humidity protection for the lane's season.
4. **ISPM-15 certificate** for any wood packaging.
5. The **container photo manifest** on dispatch.

## The result

Run all four stages and shipment damage stays **under 0.4%** even across a 35-day haul and multiple port
handovers — versus an industry average near 2%. On a container of hardware and decor, that gap is real
money and real reputation with your own customers.
