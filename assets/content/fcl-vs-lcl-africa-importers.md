# FCL vs LCL: the real break-even for African importers

Every importer asks it eventually: do I book a full container (FCL) or consolidate a part-load (LCL)?
The honest answer is a break-even calculation, not a rule of thumb — but once you understand the levers,
the decision is quick. Here is how we work it through with buyers on the India→Africa lanes.

## The two options, plainly

- **FCL (Full Container Load):** you book a whole 20' or 40' container. You pay a flat rate for the box
  regardless of how full it is. You control loading, sealing and timing.
- **LCL (Less than Container Load):** your cargo shares a container with others. You pay per
  **CBM (cubic metre)** or per tonne, whichever is greater. A consolidator handles grouping and
  de-grouping at destination.

## The capacity you're paying for

- 20' container ≈ **28–33 CBM** usable.
- 40' container ≈ **58–66 CBM** usable.

## The break-even logic

LCL is charged per CBM; FCL is a flat box price. So there is a volume at which a full LCL bill equals the
FCL box price. Below it, LCL is cheaper; above it, FCL wins — and FCL keeps getting cheaper *per unit* as
you fill the box.

A practical rule on the Mundra→West/East Africa lanes:

- **Under ~12–15 CBM:** LCL is usually cheaper.
- **~15–18 CBM:** the crossover zone — compare actual quotes.
- **Over ~18 CBM:** book a 20' FCL; over ~32 CBM, a 40'.

But price per CBM is not the whole story. **LCL carries hidden costs** that the headline rate hides.

## The costs LCL hides

- **Destination CFS/de-grouping charges** at Apapa, Mombasa, Durban etc. — often a surprise line item.
- **Higher damage risk:** more handling, mixed cargo, more touch points. (Our damage rate is <0.4% — but
  consolidated LCL is structurally riskier than a box we block-and-brace ourselves.)
- **Slower release:** the whole consolidated container must clear before your portion de-groups.
- **Documentation friction:** shared B/L arrangements add steps.

So the *effective* break-even is lower than the pure freight math suggests. Many importers find FCL worth
it from ~15 CBM once damage, demurrage and de-grouping are counted.

## The lever most importers miss: mixed-category FCL

The reason a distributor thinks they "only have an LCL's worth" is usually that they're thinking per
SKU or per category. **Consolidate across categories from one supplier** — brassware from Muradabad,
locks from Aligarh, bathroom hardware and chandeliers — and a 20' or even 40' FCL fills quickly under a
single PO and one B/L. You get FCL economics and control without over-buying any single line.

## A simple decision sheet

| Your volume | Recommended | Why |
|---|---|---|
| < 12 CBM, one-off | LCL | freight math favours it |
| 12–18 CBM | Compare quotes | crossover zone; weigh hidden LCL costs |
| 15–32 CBM | 20' FCL | control, lower damage, simpler clearance |
| 32–66 CBM | 40' FCL | best per-unit economics |
| Multi-category | Mixed FCL | fill the box across hubs, one B/L |

## The takeaway

Don't choose on freight rate alone. Count demurrage, de-grouping and damage, and check whether a
mixed-category load gets you to FCL. For most growing African distributors, a **mixed 20' or 40' FCL**
under one PO is the sweet spot — better economics, lower risk, simpler customs.
