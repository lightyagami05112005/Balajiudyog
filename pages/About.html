02 / Our story

Founded in Noida — sharpened by twenty-five years of Global trade.

Balaji Udyog began in 1998 as a brassware export house operating out of a single office in Noida. The first container left for Lagos in 2001. The second, six months later, to Mombasa. By 2010 we were running mixed-category shipments out of Mundra Port to every major Global coast.

Today we are a fully-integrated export company: a strong supplier network and vendor management expertise, a global export team staffed in English, in-house QC inspectors, and direct relationships with the freight forwarders who actually keep your container moving.

What hasn't changed: every order is owned by a named export manager from the day you inquire to the day your shipment clears customs. There is no call center. There is no general inbox.

We don't sell containers. We move relationships — between Indian craftspeople and global retailers, both of whom we've known for decades.

04 / Principles

Four standards we
don't compromise on.

These aren't taglines. They're the reason our largest customer in Lagos has been with us for nineteen years and counting.

/ 01

Named accountability

Every order has one export manager — name, photo, direct WhatsApp. No tickets. No queues. No "let me check and get back to you."

/ 02

Documentation discipline

B/L, CO, packing list, commercial invoice — every document on every shipment is double-checked against your destination port's customs format before dispatch.

/ 03

Factory transparency

You can name the factory in your PO. You can visit it. You can request weekly production photos. Nothing about the supply chain is hidden.

/ 04

Price honesty

No commission structure. Our margin is a flat documented percentage on every line item. Ask and we'll send the BOM.

/ 05

Global first

We don't take orders we can't ship. We don't promise dates the port won't keep. We don't quote rates we can't honour for 90 days.

/ 06

Long-term partnership

Our top ten customers have been with us an average of fourteen years. We optimise for the next decade, not the next quarter.
