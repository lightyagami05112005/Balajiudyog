# SONCAP, Form M & PAAR: clearing imported hardware at Apapa

Most hardware containers that get stuck in Nigeria are not stuck because of the goods. They are stuck
because of paperwork sequencing. Nigeria's import regime is document-driven and order-sensitive: do the
steps in the wrong order, and the container waits. This is a plain-language walk-through for an importer
bringing hardware, brassware or bathroom fittings into Apapa or Tin Can Island.

## The three documents that matter

**1. Form M.** Before anything ships, the importer opens a Form M through their Nigerian bank on the
Trade Monitoring System. It declares the goods, value and supplier. Nothing should leave the origin port
before the Form M is valid — a common, expensive mistake.

**2. SONCAP (Standards Organisation of Nigeria Conformity Assessment Programme).** Regulated products —
which include most hardware, electrical and building items — need a **Product Certificate (PC)** for the
specific goods and finally a **SONCAP Certificate (SC)** issued against the shipment. The PC is obtained
on the exporter's side through an accredited body (e.g. SGS, Intertek, Cotecna) based on test
reports/specifications.

**3. PAAR (Pre-Arrival Assessment Report).** Issued by Nigeria Customs against the Form M and the final
documents. The PAAR is what customs uses to assess duty and release. No PAAR, no smooth release.

## The correct sequence (this is the whole game)

```
1. Importer opens Form M (bank / TMS)            ← before shipment
2. Exporter obtains SONCAP Product Certificate    ← against goods/specs
3. Goods produced, inspected, documents drafted
4. SONCAP Certificate (SC) issued for the shipment
5. Shipment + final docs → PAAR issued by Customs
6. Arrival at Apapa → duty paid → release
```

Get steps 1–4 wrong-ordered and you create the classic Apapa delay. A competent Indian export house
runs steps 2–4 on its side and **validates every document against this sequence before the container
leaves Mundra.**

## The documents that travel with the container

- Commercial invoice (matching the Form M value)
- Packing list (carton-level, matching the invoice)
- Bill of Lading (clean on board)
- Certificate of Origin (DGFT, India)
- SONCAP Certificate (SC)
- Inspection / test reports underpinning the PC

Consistency across these is critical: the value on the invoice, Form M and PAAR must reconcile, and the
packing list must match the carton count loaded. Mismatches are the second-biggest cause of delay after
sequencing.

## Practical tips for the importer

- **Open the Form M early** and send the exporter the Form M number so the invoice can reference it.
- **Confirm your HS codes** with your agent before production — they drive duty and the PC scope.
- **Ask the exporter for the PC up front** for regulated lines; don't let it become a post-shipment
  scramble.
- **Match values exactly** across all documents. Round numbers cleanly and keep one source of truth.
- **Use a clearing agent who knows your product category** at your specific terminal.

## Where a good exporter carries the load

An export house experienced on the Nigeria lane should: obtain the SONCAP PC for regulated lines,
issue a packing list that reconciles to the loaded cartons, provide a clean B/L and DGFT CO, and hand
your agent a document pack that lines up with your Form M. That is the difference between a 3-day release
and a 3-week one.
