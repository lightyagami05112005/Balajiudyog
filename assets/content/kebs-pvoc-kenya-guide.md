# KEBS-PVoC for Kenyan importers, in plain language

Kenya's Pre-Export Verification of Conformity (PVoC) programme, run by the Kenya Bureau of Standards
(KEBS), is the gate every regulated import passes through before it can clear Mombasa. It is
**verification at origin** — the checking happens in the exporter's country, not at the Kenyan port — so
the work and the timing sit largely on the supplier's side. Here is what it means in practice for a
Kenyan importer sourcing hardware, brassware or decor from India.

## What PVoC actually is

Before shipment, the goods are verified against the applicable standards by a KEBS-appointed agent —
**Intertek, SGS or Bureau Veritas** are the common ones. On success, a **Certificate of Conformity (CoC)**
is issued. Without a CoC, the consignment faces destination inspection, penalties and delay at Mombasa.
The CoC is the document your clearing agent needs.

## The three PVoC routes

KEBS recognises three routes, chosen by how often you import the product:

- **Route A — consignment-by-consignment.** Each shipment is inspected/tested and gets its own CoC.
  Best for one-off or first-time imports. Slowest per-shipment.
- **Route B — registered products.** Frequent importers register the product (valid ~1 year). Lighter
  per-shipment verification. Good for steady SKUs.
- **Route C — licensed products.** For high-volume, consistent manufacturers; product is licensed
  (longer validity). Lightest per-shipment touch.

A distributor importing the same lines repeatedly should move to **Route B** quickly — it cuts cost and
time on every subsequent container.

## The sequence

```
1. Importer/exporter agree goods + standards + HS codes
2. Exporter books PVoC with the KEBS agent (Intertek/SGS/BV) at origin
3. Physical inspection &/or testing of the goods (pre-shipment)
4. Certificate of Conformity (CoC) issued
5. Shipment to Mombasa with CoC + commercial docs
6. Clearance: import declaration (IDF), duty, release
```

On the importer's side you'll also handle the **IDF (Import Declaration Form)** and duty; your agent
manages these against the CoC and invoice.

## Documents that travel

- Commercial invoice + packing list (reconciled)
- Bill of Lading (clean)
- Certificate of Origin (DGFT)
- **KEBS Certificate of Conformity (CoC)**
- Test reports / specs underpinning the verification

## Practical tips

- **Decide the route early.** If you'll reorder, push the exporter toward Route B registration.
- **Lock HS codes and standards** before production — they define the test scope.
- **Mombasa free time is finite.** Verification at origin is what keeps you inside it; a missing CoC is
  what blows past it.
- **Pick an agent and stick with it** across shipments for consistency.

## Where the exporter earns their keep

A supplier experienced on the Kenya lane books the PVoC with the right agent, schedules inspection
around the production calendar (so it doesn't add weeks), gets the CoC issued cleanly, and hands your
clearing agent a document pack that reconciles. Transit Mundra→Mombasa is short (~12–16 days); the
paperwork, not the sea, is what usually decides your clearance speed.
